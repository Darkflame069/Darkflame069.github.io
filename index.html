Approach

Every ambiguous ask gets turned into criteria before it becomes a roadmap item.

01

Score before you build

Vague opportunities get run through a scoring matrix before they earn a spot on the roadmap. Market fit, feasibility, and risk get made explicit instead of assumed.

02

Disqualify fast

Knowing what not to pursue is as valuable as knowing what to build. I write rejection criteria as deliberately as acceptance criteria.

03

Name the gaps

Cross-functional work stalls when ownership is implicit, so I map who owns what out loud before it turns into a missed sprint.

04

Treat feedback as input

A strategy doc isn't done when it's shared. It's done once the real gaps in it, like pricing, validation, and sizing, get closed.

GTM Strategy for Underserved Verticals

Aunix · Strategy
Context

Aunix needed a beachhead market among industries with heavy workflow fragmentation. Going too broad would have diluted positioning and sales focus.

Approach
  • Built an industry scoring matrix to rank manufacturing, professional services, and healthcare against fit criteria
  • Designed a litmus-test framework to quickly validate whether a given account matched the target profile
  • Positioned against competitors around natural-language interfaces and rapid deployment
Outcome

Review feedback flagged real gaps in market sizing, pricing specificity, risk assessment, and customer validation. Those became the next round of work: structured customer interviews, tiered pricing, unit economics, and a 90-day execution roadmap.

Product Analytics Dashboard

Aunix · Analytics
Context

The team had strategy decks but no shared, living view of how the product was actually being used. Adoption, retention, and feature usage all lived in separate spreadsheets.

Approach

Built a multi-page analytics dashboard in Appsmith with separate views for adoption, retention, and usage, combining KPI summaries with bar and pie charts per page so different stakeholders could check their own slice without digging through raw data.
